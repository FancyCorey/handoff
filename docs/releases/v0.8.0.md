## What's changed

- **Moves work without the same Wi-Fi, when your Android devices are close together.** Until now, both devices had to be on the same network. Now, when Wi-Fi can't reach the other device, Handoff reaches it over Bluetooth instead, within about 10 meters (30 feet). It uses the same encryption as over Wi-Fi, and only your own linked devices can connect. The other device shows as **Nearby** in **Your devices**. Moves this way take a few seconds longer.
  - It's on by default. **Settings → Reach devices over Bluetooth too** turns it off.
  - Android to Android only for now. Windows PCs still need the same network.
  - Handoff looks for your other devices over Bluetooth only during a move that Wi-Fi couldn't reach, and briefly while the app is open (not while audio is playing). It never looks for any other Bluetooth devices, and doesn't use your location.
  - No new permission to allow: it's part of "Nearby devices", which Handoff already has.
- **Clearer help when a move fails.** If the other device is on a different Wi-Fi and also out of Bluetooth range, **Help and diagnostics** says so and suggests bringing the devices closer or joining the same Wi-Fi.
- **The Bluetooth log in Help and diagnostics** now also notes when Handoff found a linked device nearby, or couldn't connect to it.

## Which file do I need?

| Your device | Download |
|---|---|
| Android phone or tablet (Android 12 or newer) | **Handoff-0.8.0.apk** |
| Windows 10 or 11 PC | **Handoff-0.8.0-setup.exe** (recommended) or **Handoff-0.8.0.msi** |
| Windows, without installing anything | **Handoff-0.8.0-windows-portable.zip**: unzip it and run `Handoff.exe` |

**Already using Handoff?** Open **Settings → Check for updates** (Android) or **Settings → Check** (Windows) and choose **Update**. Your linked devices and headphones stay as they are. For Bluetooth nearby, update **both** Android devices.

## Good to know

- **Android** asks you to allow installing apps from your browser or file manager the first time you open the APK.
- **Windows** may warn "Windows protected your PC" because Handoff isn't code-signed yet. Choose **More info → Run anyway**. On PCs with *Smart App Control* switched on, Windows doesn't offer that option yet.
- `SHA256SUMS.txt` lists the fingerprint of every app file, so you can check a download hasn't been altered. The `-sbom.cdx.json` files list every library the apps contain.
