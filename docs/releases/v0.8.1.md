## What's changed

- **Moves make sure the old device has really let go.** Headphones that connect to one device at a time can break up (crackling, cut-outs, especially with AAC) if the device they left reconnects to them in the background while the new one plays. Two checks now prevent that:
  - **Settling.** After connecting, Handoff waits about two seconds and checks that the headphones are still connected and that the device they came from confirms again that it has let go. Anything that reconnected there in the meantime is let go again. If the headphones dropped right after connecting, Handoff connects them again. The move shows "Making sure it worked…" a little longer while this happens.
  - **Guard.** For a minute after letting go, if the device they came from picks the headphones up again by itself, it lets go again within a few seconds. It only does this once the new device has confirmed it has them, so it never undoes your own choice: it stays out of the way if you cancel a move, if a move fails, if you move them back with Handoff (each move starts a fresh guard on the device it leaves), or if you switch them back from Bluetooth settings.
- **Help and diagnostics** notes both in its report: when a device let go again while the move settled, and when the headphones came back by themselves and were let go again.

Update **both** devices.

## Which file do I need?

| Your device | Download |
|---|---|
| Android phone or tablet (Android 12 or newer) | **Handoff-0.8.1.apk** |
| Windows 10 or 11 PC | **Handoff-0.8.1-setup.exe** (recommended) or **Handoff-0.8.1.msi** |
| Windows, without installing anything | **Handoff-0.8.1-windows-portable.zip**: unzip it and run `Handoff.exe` |

**Already using Handoff?** Open **Settings → Check for updates** (Android) or **Settings → Check** (Windows) and choose **Update**. Your linked devices and headphones stay as they are.

## Good to know

- **Android** asks you to allow installing apps from your browser or file manager the first time you open the APK.
- **Windows** may warn "Windows protected your PC" because Handoff isn't code-signed yet. Choose **More info → Run anyway**. On PCs with *Smart App Control* switched on, Windows doesn't offer that option yet.
- `SHA256SUMS.txt` lists the fingerprint of every app file, so you can check a download hasn't been altered. The `-sbom.cdx.json` files list every library the apps contain.
