## What's changed

- **Moves work more often when the other device has been quiet.** If Handoff hadn't heard from the device that had your headphones last, it used to connect directly straight away, and headphones that connect to one device at a time (many neckbands and earbuds) refuse that while they're still connected elsewhere. Handoff now asks that device to let go first, and only connects directly if it doesn't answer.
- **It says why a move didn't work.** When the other device doesn't answer, Handoff now says the headphones are probably still connected to it, and how to fix that: open Handoff there, or choose **Stay reachable** on it so it can answer while it's locked. The reason is at the top of the screen, the steps fold away under it, and **Help and diagnostics** is one tap away.
- **Long device names fit.** Names under the picture on the move screen no longer get cut off at the edge.
- **Better on tablets.** Screens keep a readable width instead of stretching across the whole display.

## Which file do I need?

| Your device | Download |
|---|---|
| Android phone or tablet (Android 12 or newer) | **Handoff-0.7.2.apk** |
| Windows 10 or 11 PC | **Handoff-0.7.2-setup.exe** (recommended) or **Handoff-0.7.2.msi** |
| Windows, without installing anything | **Handoff-0.7.2-windows-portable.zip**: unzip it and run `Handoff.exe` |

**Already using Handoff?** Open **Settings → Check for updates** (Android) or **Settings → Check** (Windows) and choose **Update**. Your linked devices and headphones stay as they are.

**Tip:** for moves to work while your other phone or tablet is locked, open Handoff on it and choose **Settings → When this phone is locked → Stay reachable**.

## Good to know

- **Android** asks you to allow installing apps from your browser or file manager the first time you open the APK.
- **Windows** may warn "Windows protected your PC" because Handoff isn't code-signed yet. Choose **More info → Run anyway**. On PCs with *Smart App Control* switched on, Windows doesn't offer that option yet.
- `SHA256SUMS.txt` lists the fingerprint of every app file, so you can check a download hasn't been altered. The `-sbom.cdx.json` files list every library the apps contain.
